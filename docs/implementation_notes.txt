INVERTED PENDULUM ON A CART - SWING-UP + DISCRETE PID  (MATLAB GUI)
===================================================================

FILES
-----
invertedPendulumGUI.m          the GUI + simulation (single self-contained m-file)
images/swing_up_t0.70s.png         GUI snapshot at t = 0.70 s (swing-up phase)
images/balanced_t3.00s.png         GUI snapshot at t = 3.00 s (balanced, cart at origin)
docs/implementation_notes.txt     this file

Tested with MATLAB R2024b, base MATLAB only (no toolboxes).
R2019b or newer is recommended (yyaxis, getframe).


1. HOW TO USE
-------------
1. Put invertedPendulumGUI.m in the MATLAB current folder and type:

       invertedPendulumGUI

2. Initial angle: type a value (degrees) or use the slider.
   Range is -180 ... +180 deg. 0 = upright, +/-180 = hanging straight down,
   positive = leaning toward +x (right).
3. PID gains (optional): edit Kp, Ki, Kd of the ANGLE PID and of the
   POSITION PID. These act in BALANCE mode. "Defaults" restores them.
4. Press START. Whatever the starting angle, the cart moves so that the bob
   ends up balanced ABOVE the cart, with the cart back at x = 0.
   STOP aborts, Reset clears the plots, Snapshot saves the window as PNG.
5. Display:
   - left: animation. The bob is RED during swing-up and GREEN once the PID
     has taken over; the text box shows t, theta, x, force and the mode.
   - top right: kinetic (red), potential (blue) and total (black) energy
     in ONE graph
   - middle right: pendulum angle (left axis, deg, wrapped to +/-180) and
     cart position (right axis, cm)
   - bottom: status line with hand-over time, settling times, peak force.

Batch use, e.g. to reproduce the snapshots:

   invertedPendulumGUI('InitialAngle',180,'AutoStart',true,'SnapshotTimes',[0.7 3.0])

Other options: 'SnapshotDir', 'Speed' (animation speed), 'CloseWhenDone'.


2. SYSTEM MODEL
---------------
Cart M = 3 kg (horizontal motion only), massless rod L = 0.5 m, bob m = 1.5 kg,
g = 9.81 m/s^2. theta is measured from the upright vertical. Full NONLINEAR
equations are used (no small-angle approximation):

   (M + m sin^2(th)) x'' = F + m L th'^2 sin(th) - m g sin(th) cos(th)
   L th'' = g sin(th) - x'' cos(th)

Energies (reference level = hinge height):
   KE = 1/2 M xd^2 + 1/2 m [ (xd + L cos(th) thd)^2 + (L sin(th) thd)^2 ]
   PE = m g L cos(th)           E = KE + PE
E is not conserved: the cart force does work on the pendulum. It rises from
-m g L (hanging) to +m g L = 7.36 J (upright, at rest) and stays there.


3. CONTROL STRATEGY (two modes)
-------------------------------
Sample time Ts = 5 ms (200 Hz), force held constant between samples (ZOH),
force limited to +/-1000 N. The plant is integrated with RK4.

MODE 1 - SWING-UP (energy shaping)
   The pendulum's own energy is Ep = 1/2 m L^2 thd^2 + m g L cos(th); the goal
   is Ed = m g L. Since dEp/dt = -m L thd cos(th) x'', choosing the cart
   acceleration
        a = k (Ep - Ed) sign(thd cos th) - kx x - kv xd      (|a| <= 10 m/s^2)
   pumps energy in until the pendulum arrives at the top. The kx, kv terms keep
   the cart near the centre of the track (it stays within about +/-1 m). The
   acceleration is converted to a force with the nonlinear model:
        F = (M + m sin^2 th) a - m L thd^2 sin th + m g sin th cos th
   If the pendulum hangs perfectly still (theta = 180 deg), a small rule
   (sign(0) -> +1) gives the initial kick.

MODE 2 - BALANCE (discrete PID, default gains)
   F[k] = PID_angle(theta[k]) + PID_position(x[k])
   PID:  u = Kp e + Ki Ts sum(e) + Kd (e[k]-e[k-1])/Ts
   error = measurement - reference(0), so all gains are positive.
   Position PID has the same sign by design: to move the cart left the system
   must first push it right so the pendulum leans left.

              Kp      Ki     Kd
   Angle      1941    20     413
   Position   1817    50     700

HAND-OVER: swing-up -> PID when |theta| < 20.5 deg, |thetadot| < 8 rad/s and
|Ep - Ed| < 0.5 Ed (integrators are reset). If |theta| ever exceeds 45 deg in
balance mode, control falls back to swing-up. The angle is wrapped to
(-180, 180] deg for the controller.


4. RESULTS (default settings, simulation length 8 s)
----------------------------------------------------
 start angle   PID takes over   pendulum within +/-0.5 deg   cart within +/-2 cm   peak |x|   peak |F|
 15 deg        0.00 s           0.92 s                        0.58 s               30 cm      508 N
 20 deg        0.00 s           1.01 s                        0.46 s               44 cm      678 N
 90 deg        3.79 s           4.76 s                        4.20 s               86 cm      309 N
 -135 deg      2.69 s           3.60 s                        3.03 s               95 cm      150 N
 180 deg       1.31 s           2.30 s                        1.50 s               95 cm       54 N

 * Starts inside the PID's capture range (up to about 20 deg) skip swing-up
   and are balanced in about 1 s, as in the first version.
 * A start angle sweep from -180 to +180 deg in 10 deg steps (12 s runs,
   separate test script) balanced successfully every time; worst case about
   5 s, cart always within +/-1 m.
 * Final residuals: theta ~ 0, |x| < 0.2 cm.

Limitations:
 * The 1 s balance time only holds for small starts (<= ~20 deg); a swing-up
   from far away physically needs a few seconds because the pendulum must
   gain energy first.
 * Swing-up gains were tuned for these masses and length (M=3, m=1.5, L=0.5)
   and the +/-1.2 m display range. If you change them, re-tune. A fixed
   track length is not enforced by physics; it is kept by the centring terms.
 * Perfect model and perfect sensing are assumed (no noise or delay).
